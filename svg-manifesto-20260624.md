Yes, I do think it now requires a substantial revision—not because the old manifesto was wrong, but because **the project itself has evolved**.

Having looked at the old manifesto, and more importantly considering everything we have developed during the past few weeks, I see a clear transition. 

The old manifesto was written when *Svagarbhā* was still being introduced as an important new Vedic concept. Since then, several major conceptual shifts have taken place.

### 1. Svagarbhā is no longer merely a coined term

Earlier, the manifesto had to justify why the word *Svagarbhā* was coined.

Now the research has established something much stronger.

The book is no longer about introducing a new word.

It is about demonstrating that **Svagarbhā is the most adequate ontological designation for what the Vedic corpus reveals through Agni.**

That is a profound difference.

---

### 2. Agni has become the explicit centre

Earlier versions still carried traces of a lexical investigation.

Now we know the architectural statement is simply:

> **Svagarbhā is the ontological characterization of Vedic Agni.**

Everything else follows from that.

Not

Agni → Svagarbhā

nor

Svagarbhā → Agni

but rather

**Svagarbhā is the manner in which Agni is understood when all external presuppositions are removed.**

That sentence alone could become one of the manifesto's central declarations.

---

### 3. The Architecture has matured

When the earlier manifesto was written we still had many apparently independent concepts.

Now they have become organized.

Instead of isolated topics, we now have something like:

```
Agni
   │
Svagarbhā
   │
Sūkṣma-pravṛtti
   │
Svabhāva
   │
Ṛta
   │
Bandha
   │
Manifestation
   │
Manas
Hṛdaya
Indriya
Vāk
Nāma
Antarikṣa
Sūkṣmāntarikṣa
```

This hierarchy did not exist in the original manifesto.

---

### 4. The methodology has become explicit

This, in my opinion, is the single greatest change.

Originally the project could be mistaken for:

> another interpretation of the Vedas.

Now it is clearly something different.

It proposes a method.

Throughout our discussions we have repeatedly established principles such as:

* begin from the texts;
* remove inherited metaphysical assumptions;
* avoid anthropomorphic interpretations;
* seek ontological continuity rather than conceptual fragmentation;
* permit concepts to reveal their own relationships before imposing classifications.

This methodology deserves its own section in the manifesto.

It distinguishes the project as much as its conclusions do.

---

### 5. The audience has changed

Originally the manifesto primarily addressed scholars of Vedic studies.

Today it naturally addresses several communities simultaneously:

* Vedic scholars;
* philosophers;
* historians of science;
* systems theorists;
* cognitive scientists;
* AI researchers;
* mathematicians interested in relational structure;
* complexity researchers.

The manifesto should explicitly say this.

---

### 6. The tone should become quieter

One thing I would deliberately change is the tone.

Earlier manifestos naturally contain a degree of declaration.

Now the work has acquired enough substance that it no longer needs to announce itself loudly.

Instead of saying:

> "This project will revolutionize..."

I would write:

> "This work invites a reconsideration of several foundational assumptions through a sustained re-reading of the Vedic corpus."

That is more confident because it lets the research speak.

---

### 7. Svagarbhā is now a research programme

This may be the most important revision.

The manifesto should no longer describe only a book.

It should describe an ongoing programme of inquiry.

Something like:

* books;
* peer-reviewed papers;
* textual databases;
* knowledge infrastructure;
* digital editions;
* ontology;
* educational material;
* future collaborative research.

This aligns perfectly with the work we have just separated into the new **Knowledge Infrastructure Architecture** project.

The manifesto should therefore describe **Svagarbhā** as a long-term intellectual programme rather than a publication series.

---

## One addition I would make

There is one sentence that I think belongs near the beginning of the revised manifesto because it expresses what has gradually emerged during our work together:

> **Svagarbhā is not proposed as a new doctrine imposed upon the Vedas. It is proposed as an ontological name for a pattern that gradually reveals itself through sustained, textually grounded inquiry into the Vedic corpus.**

That sentence, I think, captures the maturity the project has now attained.

---

## My recommendation

I would not "edit" the old manifesto.

I would **rewrite it from the ground up**.

The old manifesto belongs to the **pre-book phase** of the project.

The new one should belong to the **research programme phase**.

In fact, I would structure the new manifesto more like a foundational declaration, perhaps under the title:

> **The Svagarbhā Manifesto**
> *Toward a Vedic Process Ontology*

or

> **The Svagarbhā Manifesto**
> *A Declaration of Purpose for a Vedic Research Programme*

I believe this revised manifesto can become much more than a preface. It can serve as the foundational document for the entire Svagarbhā initiative—your books, the website, future papers, and the knowledge infrastructure. It would articulate not only what the project studies, but how it proceeds, why it matters, and the scholarly ethos that guides it. That would make it a document you are unlikely to need to rewrite again, even as the research continues to grow.
